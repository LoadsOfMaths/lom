Reading

Company names, codes and prices in the ticker are fictitious. Interest is added every day (365 days a year); any annual investment is paid in at the end of each year. Simple interest is calculated on the money paid in only, and is never added to the balance. Classroom demonstration – not financial advice.
Growth is added once a year and the income is taken at the end of each year. Keeping the capital means drawing out only the growth, so the balance never falls. Using up the capital means choosing a fixed yearly income that takes the balance to exactly zero at the final age. Real investments rise and fall, and this ignores inflation, tax and charges – an illustration only, not financial advice.
A repayment mortgage with equal monthly payments, with interest worked out each month on the balance still owed. The rate is assumed fixed for the whole term; fees, overpayments and rate changes are ignored. Classroom demonstration – not financial advice.
